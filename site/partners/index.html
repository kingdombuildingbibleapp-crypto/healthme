Discharge instructions end at the door. Patients' next decision happens in the grocery aisle.

HealthyScan turns a patient's own diagnoses, allergies, medications and labs, imported from MyChart with their permission, into a green, yellow or red verdict on any product they scan. It's built to support diet adherence after discharge and between visits, without adding work for nurses.

How it works in your workflow

  1. 1Recommend at discharge

    The nurse or dietitian hands over a QR card or mentions it in the After Visit Summary. No EHR order or build is needed.

  2. 2Patient connects MyChart

    The patient signs in to your patient portal, which uses SMART on FHIR, and approves read-only access. Diagnoses, allergies, medications and recent labs import to their phone.

  3. 3Patient reviews

    Every finding shows the line of the record it came from. The patient confirms or unchecks each one. Nothing is added without approval.

  4. 4Scan anything

    Food, supplements, skincare, cleaners. Each product gets a verdict for the patient, and for every child or dependent on the same phone.

  5. 5Optional, phase 2: outcomes to the care team

    With patient consent and a BAA, aggregate adherence signals can be reported to care managers. This is not part of the MVP.

HIPAA and patient data protection

Where data lives

  • On the patient's device only. Profiles, conditions and scan history are stored in the phone's browser storage. HealthyScan runs no database and no user accounts.
  • Records are read on the device. PDFs, photos (text recognition), CCD/XML and FHIR data are parsed in the browser. The record text is discarded after analysis and only approved findings are kept.
  • Product lookups send only a barcode number to open product databases, never health information.
  • No trackers. No analytics, ad pixels or third-party fonts or scripts. This is enforced by a strict Content-Security-Policy and HSTS.
  • Read-only EHR access. The OAuth token is used once for import and then cleared. HealthyScan never writes to the EHR.

Epic and FHIR integration

How it connects

  • SMART on FHIR R4 standalone patient launch. OAuth 2.0 authorization code flow with PKCE, as a public client. The patient signs in through your MyChart.
  • Read-only USCDI / US Core data. No custom interfaces, HL7 v2 feeds or write-back are needed for the MVP.
  • Epic route: register as a patient-facing app on fhir.epic.com with USCDI read scopes. Epic documents automatic client-ID distribution to participating Epic organizations for this app type, so a Novant or Atrium MyChart patient could connect without a site-by-site build. Each health system's own review still applies.
  • Phase 2 options: a provider-launched SMART app inside Epic so clinicians can attach a "nutrition prescription" at discharge, and FHIR write-back of patient-reported data, each scoped with your IT team.

Proposed 90-day pilot

Shaped for innovation teams that test whether a service is desirable, feasible and viable before scaling. Atrium Health's Design for Impact process is one example.

Scope

  • One service line: heart failure transitional care, or a pediatric food-allergy clinic
  • 150–300 consenting patients at discharge or visit
  • Matched comparison cohort from the same unit
  • No EHR build. Patients connect MyChart themselves

Measures

  • 30-day readmission and ED revisit rate, from your data warehouse
  • Enrollment and 30/60/90-day active use
  • Patient-reported confidence choosing foods
  • Nurse discharge-teaching minutes, measured by time study

Timeline

  • Weeks 0–2: security review, BAA if sponsored, staff huddle
  • Weeks 2–10: enrollment and use
  • Weeks 10–13: outcome readout and go or no-go

Readiness checklist

Done

  • Live MVP at healthyscan.net, installable on iPhone and Android
  • 318 conditions, 23 medication groups, child age rules
  • On-device record import: PDF, photo, CCD/XML, FHIR

Glossary

Acronyms used in this brief and in HealthyScan.

Healthcare data and interoperability

EHR
Electronic Health Record — The hospital's patient record system, such as Epic
FHIR
Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources — The modern web standard for exchanging health data. HealthyScan reads records this way
R4
FHIR Release 4 — The FHIR version used by Epic and most U.S. health systems
SMART
Substitutable Medical Applications, Reusable Technologies — The standard way apps sign patients in to an EHR ("SMART on FHIR")
HL7
Health Level Seven — The standards body behind FHIR. HL7 v2 is an older message format HealthyScan doesn't need
US Core
US Core Implementation Guide — The U.S. rules for which FHIR data fields systems must support
USCDI
United States Core Data for Interoperability — The federally required set of patient data that EHRs must share
ICD-10
International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision — Diagnosis codes in medical records, like E11.9 for type 2 diabetes
CCD / C-CDA
Continuity of Care Document / Consolidated Clinical Document Architecture — Downloadable XML record summaries from patient portals
API
Application Programming Interface — A way for software to request data from other software
MyChart
Epic's patient portal — Used by Novant Health, Atrium Health and many others

Privacy, security and regulation

HIPAA
Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act — The federal health privacy law
PHI
Protected Health Information — Health data that HIPAA protects
BAA
Business Associate Agreement — The contract a hospital signs with a vendor that handles PHI for it
HHS
U.S. Department of Health and Human Services — Enforces HIPAA through its Office for Civil Rights (OCR)
FTC
Federal Trade Commission — Regulates privacy for health apps that HIPAA doesn't cover
HBNR
Health Breach Notification Rule — The FTC rule requiring breach notices within 60 days
FDA
Food and Drug Administration — Regulates medical devices, including software that diagnoses or treats
OAuth 2.0
Open Authorization — The secure sign-in standard SMART uses
PKCE
Proof Key for Code Exchange — An OAuth safeguard that protects sign-in on phones
CSP
Content-Security-Policy — A browser setting that blocks unapproved scripts and trackers
HSTS
HTTP Strict Transport Security — Forces browsers to always use an encrypted connection
HTTPS / TLS
Hypertext Transfer Protocol Secure / Transport Layer Security — The encrypted web connection behind the padlock in the browser
SOC 2
System and Organization Controls 2 — An independent security audit hospitals often require
WCAG 2.1 AA
Web Content Accessibility Guidelines, level AA — The accessibility standard hospitals expect
